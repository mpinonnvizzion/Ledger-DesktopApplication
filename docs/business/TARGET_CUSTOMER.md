# Target Customer

## Purpose

This document defines who Ledger is built for.

Every product decision, feature request, user interface improvement, and future roadmap item should be evaluated against the needs of these customers.

Building for everyone inevitably leads to building for no one. Ledger succeeds by deeply understanding a specific audience and delivering an exceptional experience for them.

---

## Primary Customer

Ledger is designed primarily for individuals and small businesses that want professional financial management software without surrendering ownership of their data.

These customers value:

* Privacy
* Local data ownership
* Long-term reliability
* Simple, predictable workflows
* Professional software that does not require a monthly subscription

They are willing to pay for high-quality software if they believe it will remain useful for years.

---

## Primary Customer Profiles

### Individual Professionals

People managing their personal finances who have outgrown spreadsheets but do not need enterprise accounting software.

Typical needs:

* Expense tracking
* Budgeting
* Financial goals
* Reports
* Investment visibility
* Simple bank reconciliation

### Freelancers

Independent workers managing both personal and business finances.

Typical needs:

* Separate workspaces
* Invoice creation
* Client management
* Expense categorization
* Tax preparation support
* Profit reporting

### Small Business Owners

Businesses with one to ten employees that need straightforward financial organization without the complexity of enterprise accounting systems.

Typical needs:

* Multiple accounts
* Vendors
* Customers
* Accounts payable
* Accounts receivable
* Receipt management
* Reporting

Ledger is intended to complement accounting workflows, not replace a CPA.

---

## Secondary Customers

Ledger should also support:

* Students learning personal finance.
* Families managing household budgets.
* Hobby businesses.
* Side hustles.
* Rental property owners.
* Consultants.
* Contractors.

These users benefit from the application's flexibility but are not the primary audience when making product decisions.

---

## Customers We Are Not Building For

Ledger is intentionally not designed for:

### Large Enterprises

Organizations requiring:

* ERP systems
* Multi-department accounting
* Complex approval workflows
* Large accounting teams
* Thousands of simultaneous users

### Professional Accounting Firms

Ledger is not intended to replace specialized accounting software used by CPAs or tax preparation firms.

### Cryptocurrency Traders

Basic cryptocurrency account tracking may exist in the future, but Ledger will not become a crypto portfolio platform or trading terminal.

### Day Traders

Ledger focuses on financial organization rather than investment execution or market analysis.

### Financial Institutions

Banks, lenders, and investment firms have requirements that extend far beyond Ledger's intended scope.

---

## Customer Values

Our customers generally value:

* Privacy over convenience
* Ownership over subscriptions
* Stability over constant redesigns
* Transparency over hidden behavior
* Speed over visual effects
* Reliability over experimental features

These values should influence every product decision.

---

## Product Promise

When someone purchases Ledger, they should feel that they own their financial software.

They should never worry that:

* Their financial data will disappear because a cloud service shuts down.
* Their subscription is required just to access their own records.
* Their workflow will suddenly change because of an unnecessary redesign.
* Their software is collecting more information than necessary.

Ledger should feel dependable, respectful, and built to last.

---

## Feature Decision Framework

Before adding a feature, ask:

1. Does this solve a meaningful problem for our primary customers?
2. Does it improve financial organization or understanding?
3. Does it preserve simplicity?
4. Does it respect user privacy?
5. Can it be maintained for years?

If the answer to any of these questions is "no," the feature should be reconsidered or postponed.

---

## Success Definition

Ledger succeeds when its users recommend it because it is:

* Easy to trust.
* Easy to understand.
* Fast to use.
* Pleasant to own.
* Reliable for years.

Our goal is not to build software with the most features.

Our goal is to build software that becomes the trusted financial hub for the people it was designed to serve.
